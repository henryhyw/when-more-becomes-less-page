01  ·  The setup

Same prompt. Different answer slot.

Probing studies of language models often vary the number of times a target word appears in a prompt, then read the model's prediction at a single slot. The implicit assumption is that the slot's position is orthogonal to how repetition affects prediction.

To test this, we hold the prefix fixed — cat cat cat … — and vary only where the model is asked to predict. Step through N below and watch the two probabilities move.

03  ·  The pattern

An inverted-U emerges at the displaced slot.

Stepping back from the worked example: when we plot mean P(target) against the number of repeated copies N on the displaced probe, every model produces a curve that rises early and then falls. The location of the peak shifts between models — some peak at N=1, others at N=10 or N=20 — but the shape is shared.

05  ·  The cause

Only exact lexical repetition does it.

We swap the N=30 target block for five alternatives. Random fillers, deletion, and an unrelated repeated token (‘table’) push P(target) below 0.001. The interesting case is the semantic-neighbour filler: thirty close neighbours of the target. They activate the semantic field — Σ P(synonyms) jumps to 0.316 in ModernBERT-large — but P(target) itself stays around 0.03.

The semantic field can be activated without reproducing the inverted-U. What the model needs is the exact string.

06  ·  Cross-lingual

It replicates in four languages.

To rule out an English- or WordPiece-specific artefact, we run the displaced probe unchanged on four multilingual checkpoints across Spanish, Chinese, German, and French, with three hand-translated frames per language. XLM-R Chinese drops out because no Chinese single-token candidate survives its BPE filter, leaving 14 (model, language) combinations and 42 (model, language, frame) cells.

07  ·  Inside

The block tightens its hold as you add copies.

At the attention level: per-target-token attention falls with N in every model — what you'd predict if a fixed budget were divided over more keys. But in the three causal LMs we probe, the total attention the model assigns to the repeated block grows with N rather than shrinks. Per-token attention drops because the budget is divided across more tokens, but the budget itself grows. ModernBERT-large — the lone masked LM in the mechanism subset — is the exception: its block budget stays flat.

Last-layer attention · F0 displaced · 4 mechanism models

Sub-1/N dilution. The dissociation is by training objective in our four-model subset: 3 CLMs grow the budget; the MLM does not.

08  ·  Where it weakens

Depth, not parameter count.

Two natural depth-vs-width contrasts make the same point. Within SmolLM2, the smaller 360 M checkpoint is deeper (32 layers) and shows a clearer inversion than the larger 1.7 B checkpoint (24 layers). And on the older BERT-tiny / mini / small family of Turc et al. (2019), the transition between absent and present sits at the depth jump from 2 to 4 layers.

Depth-vs-width contrasts · F0 displaced circle area ∝ parameters · same-family points joined

Doubling hidden size at fixed depth leaves the drop essentially unchanged. Going from 2 to 4 layers introduces the inverted-U.

09  ·  What it means

Slot position is not orthogonal to repetition.

The diagnostic is general. Any controlled probing study that varies how often a target token appears in a prompt — cloze probes of factual knowledge, semantic-priming studies, in-context-learning analyses that vary demonstration count, RAG-style passage concatenation — should run the two-probe contrast before reading a single slot's curve as the answer.

Adjacent and displaced are different regimes, and the displaced regime is the one that breaks the priming intuition. A spot-check on a LAMA-style prompt (the answer stated upfront, an N-copy block in the middle, the question re-asked) shows the diagnostic still carries signal even when the model has a second retrieval route — though attenuated, with drops of 0.06–0.14 versus 0.42–0.69 in the main probe.

The shape we found may turn out to share a mechanism with attention-sink dynamics, or with autoregressive recency priors, or with softmax cooperation between repeated keys — we don't yet know. What we do know is that the same prompt can produce opposite curves under two reasonable readout choices. Reporting only one of them hides the effect.
